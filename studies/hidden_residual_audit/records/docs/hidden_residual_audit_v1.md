# Hidden learned-relation residual audit v1

## Status and question

This post-formal exploratory diagnostic is complete on the two frozen
development checkpoints, seeds 1901 and 1902. Its machine-readable protocol was
committed and pushed as `b815ede` before any new hidden-residual estimand was
executed. The implementation was then source-locked before the read-only run.
No network was trained or changed, no checkpoint was selected, and no formal
seed was accessed.

The question was whether v1 already contains a relation-specific episodic trace
in the response hidden state that is causally generated by retained evidence,
linearly accessible across subjects and relation identities, but suppressed by
the fixed output direction. A positive answer would favor changing the v2
readout or objective before adding a new memory mechanism.

The result is mixed and decisive for that choice:

> Retained relations cause a small, spatially localized non-additive hidden
> residual, but it is not a common correctness-aligned channel suppressed by
> `W_out`.

This rejects the simple “the local channel is already present and only needs a
new readout” account. It does not show that relation-specific information is
absent from the fast-weight state, earlier query steps, or higher-dimensional
relation-conditioned subspaces.

## Frozen estimands

For subject $s$ and canonical query edge $e=(i,j)$, the response hidden
vector field is

\[
H_s(e)=\frac{1}{2}\left[h_s(i,j)-h_s(j,i)\right].
\]

The complete-graph Hodge projector $Q$ separates each hidden unit's field
into a global gradient and non-additive residual:

\[
H_s^G=QH_s,\qquad H_s^C=(I-Q)H_s.
\]

For each support relation $r$, the registered relation-LOO replay gives

\[
\Delta C_{r\to e}
=(I-Q)\left[H_{\mathrm{intact}}(e)-H_{-r}(e)\right].
\]

The causal-local estimand compares the L2 norm of the direct
\(\Delta C_{r\to r}\) with the mean per-edge norm on queries remote from both
endpoints. LOO of a relation already removed by a subject's stable-omission
mask is the exact zero control.

Linear availability uses no regression or hyperparameter. For every evaluation
subject fold and relation, a unit matched direction is formed by averaging
correctness-aligned direct residual vectors from the other six subject folds
and the other seven relation identities. It is then evaluated on the jointly
held-out subject and relation. This prevents a relation-specific vector or a
same-subject effect from being relabeled as a shared local channel.

The same residual vectors are projected on the unit-normalized fixed output
direction. The comparison concerns directional accessibility in frozen hidden
state differences; it is not a recurrent intervention or evidence that a new
probe is used by the network.

### Positive result: retained evidence leaves a causal local residual

The direct-minus-remote residual-norm interval is above zero in both seeds, and
the stable-omission controls are exactly zero. A retained support relation
therefore leaves a causal non-additive trace in the response hidden vector
field. Its direct per-edge magnitude is about 2.4--2.5 times the remote mean.

This strengthens the v1 representation boundary: the response hidden field is
not itself a pure global potential even though its fixed output projection is
nearly additive. The effect is small in total-energy terms, however: only about
`1.2--1.6%` of relation-LOO hidden-influence energy lies in the Hodge residual.

### Negative result: there is no shared suppressed fidelity channel

The registered shared-direction diagnosis fails in both seeds. Seed 1901's
cross-validated direct correctness interval includes zero. More importantly,
the shared direction is not selectively direct rather than remote in either
seed. Its direct-minus-remote interval is negative for seed 1901 and unresolved
for seed 1902.

The fixed-readout-suppression prediction is directionally contradicted. The
unit-normalized current output direction reads more correctness-aligned direct
effect than the jointly held-out matched direction in both seeds. Although
`W_out` increases the overall gradient-energy fraction by about `0.011`, it is
not simply discarding a separate common local-fidelity axis.

### Relation heterogeneity explains the failed common direction

Six of the eight support relations have positive cross-validated direct
projections in both seeds. The maximum-distance `H>A` relation is different:
its residual direct projection is strongly correctness-opposed (`-0.338` and
`-0.216`), despite having the largest direct-local residual-norm enrichment
(`0.282` and `0.206`). `F>A` is also weak or nonselective. These are residual
components only; the competent global gradient still supports correct total
behavior.

The strongest non-additive trace is therefore not a uniform replay of direct
experience. It may encode relation-specific redundancy, innovation,
confidence correction, or a state-dependent update coordinate. The present
diagnostic distinguishes this pattern from a shared learned-pair-fidelity
channel but does not choose among those higher-dimensional alternatives.

## Theory revision

The rejected shortcut was:

\[
\text{existing hidden local axis}
\xrightarrow{\text{currently suppressed by }W_{out}}
\text{human learned-pair residual}.
\]

The supported v1 representation is instead:

\[
H_{ij}
=H^G_{ij}+H^C_{ij},
\]

where $H^G$ carries the confirmed global assembly and the small causal
$H^C$ is relation heterogeneous rather than a common fidelity channel. The
human mixed-code mechanism remains missing at the current response-state
readout level.

This negative result should redirect v2 rather than weaken the global claim.
Do not merely fit a second linear readout to the same response states. First
determine whether local experience is stored in relation-conditioned
fast-weight/support-state coordinates and either routed differently during the
query or lost before the response state.

## Next decisive test

Freeze a new one-to-two-seed read-only protocol before inspecting new
estimands. It should separate two possibilities:

1. **Relation-conditioned trace:** construct within-relation directions using
   held-out subjects, then test them on the same relation under retained versus
   stable-omitted and matched LOO conditions. Cross-relation failure together
   with within-relation success would identify a high-dimensional local code,
   not a universal output axis.
2. **Earlier-state trace:** apply the same retained/omitted and LOO contrasts to
   the generated effective write and terminal fast-weight state before query
   recurrence. Persistence there but not in a usable response direction would
   localize the missing operation to query routing; absence there would justify
   considering a new local persistent mechanism.

In parallel, the seed-2009 implementation question remains separate: test
whether complete natural-write transfer rescues donor identity when isolated
DA and eligibility transfers fail. Do not merge that operator-level
heterogeneity with the missing human local-fidelity mechanism.
